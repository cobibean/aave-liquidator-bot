Field Guide · Aave V3 Liquidation Bot

The Liquidator

A multi-chain bot that finds underwater Aave loans and liquidates them for the discount — and the story of why it was catching nothing, and how that got fixed.

Status: live on droplet · TEST_MODE Chains: 5 Updated: 2026-06-02 Audience: humans & agents

TL;DR — read this first

  • What it does: watches every Aave borrower on 5 chains; when someone's health factor drops below 1.0, it flash-loan liquidates them, takes the collateral bonus, and repays the loan — profit stays in the contract.
  • The bug we found: it ran overnight and caught 0 of ~107 real liquidations. Cause: it only looked at wallets that borrowed recently, but the wallets that get liquidated are old positions. 35 of 36 Arbitrum liquidations were invisible to it.
  • The fix: discover every borrower from each pool's deployment (not just recent), check health factors fast via Multicall3, ignore sub-$100 dust, and refuse unprofitable liquidations.
  • Right now: still in TEST_MODE (logs, never submits). Go-live is a deliberate human decision.

01 · What it does

Aave lets people borrow against collateral. If their collateral falls (or debt grows with interest) until the loan is unsafe — a health factor (HF) below 1.0 — anyone may repay part of the debt and seize collateral at a discount (the liquidation bonus, ~5–10%). That discount is the profit. This bot competes to be that "anyone."

Discover

Build the list of every wallet that has borrowed, per chain, by scanning historical Borrow events.

Watch

Check each borrower's health factor continuously, focusing on those near the danger line.

Strike

When HF < 1 and it's profitable, flash-loan the debt, liquidate, swap collateral, repay, keep the spread.

It runs 24/7 on a DigitalOcean droplet in Docker, across 5 EVM chains in one process, each on its own loop.

02 · The bug — why it caught nothing

The symptomRan overnight on 5 chains with gas funded. Caught zero liquidatable positions. Ground-truth scan of Aave's LiquidationCall events showed it wasn't bad luck: ~107 real liquidations happened that night (Arbitrum 36, Optimism 37, Base 25, Avalanche 9) — all taken by competitors.

Root cause: looking in the wrong place

The original discovery only scanned the most recent blocks of Borrow events. But a position becomes liquidatable as it ages — prices drift, interest accrues — not the moment someone borrows. So the bot's candidate list was almost entirely the wrong wallets.

The proofOf the 36 wallets liquidated on Arbitrum that night, only 1 was in the bot's 439-wallet candidate set. It would never have even looked at the other 35. On Plasma the window was ~2.7 hours → it knew of just 2 borrowers total.

03 · How it works now

Backfill borrowers (once)→ Incremental scan (every cycle)→ Multicall3 HF sweep→ Filter: HF<1 & ≥$100→ callStatic profit check→ Liquidate

① Discovery — find every borrower, then stay current

On first run, a one-time deep backfill scans Borrow events from the pool's deployment block forward, building the complete borrower set. It's resumable (checkpoints every 25 chunks, so a restart continues instead of starting over) and persisted to a Docker volume. After that, every cycle scans only the handful of new blocks since last time and merges in new borrowers — so the list is never more than seconds stale. (Avalanche & Optimism are capped at ~366 days because full deployment depth would take ~30h on public RPCs; that still covers every still-active borrower.)

② Stratified health-factor sweep — fast at scale

Checking tens of thousands of health factors every cycle doesn't scale. So:

  • Every cycle: re-check the watchlist — wallets near the danger line (HF < 1.25) and ≥$100 debt. Small and fast.
  • Every 20 cycles: a full sweep of all borrowers to refresh the watchlist.
  • Always: newly-discovered borrowers are checked the same cycle they appear.
  • All reads go through Multicall3 (one call = hundreds of health factors). Arbitrum's 13.8k-borrower full sweep is ~5s; watchlist cycles are ~1s.

③ Profitability gate — never lose money

Two layers (defense in depth): the bot simulates each liquidation with callStatic first and skips anything that would revert; and the on-chain contract enforces a minimum-profit floor at the swap, so a sandwiched/bad-price fill reverts cheaply instead of selling the bonus at a loss.

05 · The liquidator contract

AaveLiquidatorSwapRouter02.sol — owner-only, holds no funds between liquidations. The flow inside one transaction:

flashLoanSimple(debt)→ liquidationCall → receive collateral→ swap collateral → debt (V3 router)→ repay loan + premium→ keep profit
FunctionWhoWhat
triggerLiquidation(debt, amt, user, collat)ownerLiquidate using the stored minProfit floor.
triggerLiquidationWithMinProfit(…, floor)ownerLiquidate with a per-call (gas-aware) floor; enforces max(floor, stored).
executeOperation(…)Aave poolFlash-loan callback: does the liquidate→swap→repay, reverts if profit floor unmet.
setMinProfit(units)ownerSet the stored minimum-profit floor (debt-token units).
setIntermediateTokens / setPreferredFeeTierownerTune the collateral→debt swap routing.
withdrawToken(token, amt, to)ownerSweep accumulated profit out of the contract.
The hardeningThe original contract set the swap's amountOutMinimum to just the loan repayment — meaning MEV/slippage could siphon the entire bonus and the tx would still "succeed." Now it requires repayment + profitFloor, enforced at the swap boundary.

06 · How profit is protected

Profitability isn't a single number — it's gas (varies by chain & moment) vs. the bonus (a % of debt). The bot computes a floor per liquidation:

// floor (in debt-token units), enforced on-chain
floor = MIN_PROFIT_USD  +  PROFIT_SAFETY_MULTIPLE × liveGasCostUSD
liveGasCostUSD via Aave oracle (native & debt prices, 8-dp USD)

07 · Running & operating it

Where it lives

DigitalOcean droplet liquidator-solo-1 (nyc3, 2 vCPU / 4 GB), app dir /opt/aave-liquidator-bot, container aave-liquidator via Docker Compose. Borrower store persists in the named volume liquidator-data at /app/data.

Deploy an update

# from your machine — preserves the droplet's .env
rsync -az --delete --exclude .env --exclude node_modules \
  --exclude data --exclude .git ./ \
  root@<droplet-ip>:/opt/aave-liquidator-bot/
ssh root@<droplet-ip> 'cd /opt/aave-liquidator-bot && docker compose up -d --build'

Watch it

docker logs -f aave-liquidator                 # live
docker logs aave-liquidator | grep "🔥 Found"  # real catches
docker logs aave-liquidator | grep "BACKFILL complete"

Check reality (did we cover what's being liquidated?)

docker exec aave-liquidator node scripts/recentLiquidations.js
docker exec aave-liquidator node scripts/probeMeaningfulCoverage.js base 100
Live modeThe bot submits when TEST_MODE=false. That can send real transactions and spend real gas. Confirm it deliberately in the droplet .env before every restart. It is live today.

10 · Known limits & what's next

open Arbitrum contract

Public RPCs reject the contract-creation tx. Arbitrum runs the old (unhardened) contract — still protected by the bot's callStatic gate. Needs a private RPC (Alchemy/Infura) to deploy the hardened one.

open Sizeable-coverage metric

Re-measure once Avalanche/Optimism backfills finish — the proof the deep discovery worked (was ~30% at 30-day depth).

idea Per-block triggers

React on each new block (not a 15s poll) to compete with adjacent-block MEV bots. Designed, not built. Needs private/WS RPCs.

idea Infra

Single 2-vCPU box runs all 5 chains in one process; heavy backfills contend. Private RPCs + per-chain workers would harden throughput.
